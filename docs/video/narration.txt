## 1 af_sarah
This is the CityPulse Simulator.

It builds a whole smart city out of nothing. It invents thousands of sensors, and sends their readings the way a real plant would.

It can also push payments, model calls and employee requests at the other apps of our showcase.

In the next six minutes, you will start it, run a city, and break that city on purpose. Then we will see how it connects to everything else.

## 2 af_sarah
The simulator is one small web app on top of Postgres. When it starts for the first time, it prints a one-time setup code. Here it is, in the terminal.

Nobody can use the app without that code. So a new install is never open by accident.

In the browser, we type the code, and choose an admin password. Create it, and we are in.

In the real fleet, people sign in through the shared identity provider instead. The roles are admin, operator and viewer.

## 3 am_michael
Let's build a city. In this app, a city is simply a list of sensor groups. We name it demo city, and pick the fixed preset, which is always the same.

Each group says what kind of sensor, how many, and in which district. It also says how often they report, and how they talk.

That last part is the protocol: the language the device speaks to the gateway. Modbus, EtherNet IP and EtherCAT are languages of factories and utilities. So are Profinet and Profibus. Direct skips them.

The last column is the burst percentage. It says how likely a sensor is to misbehave.

This preset has one hundred seventy four sensors, and it is the same every time.

If you want cities that differ, the city generator makes ten at a time. The same seed always gives the same city.

Cities can have up to ten thousand sensors. That is how you load test the whole chain.

## 4 am_michael
Click Start city, and the city comes alive. Each protocol gets a real server on this machine.

A gateway polls them, and turns the changes into readings. Just as an edge box would in a plant.

Sensors report every ten to fifteen seconds. Until the first one has, the Modbus registers stay at zero. So let's give it a moment, and watch the readings sent counter.
[pause 9]
There it goes. Now for the proof.

Here is an ordinary third party Modbus client, asking that server for its registers. And it answers with numbers.

Six registers make two channels. Each has a value, and an update counter.

Because no CityPulse address is set, the readings are counted, but not sent anywhere.

With an address, the same readings go to CityPulse over HTTPS, in batches. There is a retry buffer, if CityPulse is slow. If it pushes back, the city waits and tries again.

And if the token is refused, the page says so in plain words.

## 5 af_heart
Next, a city that misbehaves. Real cities have bad days. A burst is a random episode, where a sensor misbehaves.

We turn the whole city up for one minute, and press burst.
[pause 4]
Now look at the behaviour card. Bursts active fills up. Corrupt values and silent updates start to rise.

The episodes are a wild spike, extra noise, a stuck value, or a sensor that goes silent. Others flood, or send garbage.

Each group's burst percentage sets how often it happens. This button turns the whole city up at once.

So later, the monitoring apps have something real to notice. And if you give the city a seed, even the chaos repeats exactly.

## 6 af_heart
Cities cover the sensors. Workloads cover everything else. A workload sends simulated traffic to one app, on a schedule you choose.

First we stop the city, and open the workloads tab.

We create a workload called shop telemetry, of kind PagerLens telemetry. It plays a small online shop, reporting to PagerLens, our incident tool.

We pick the healthy baseline, every five seconds, then save and start.
[pause 5]
The blue banner says generate only. With no address, it only generates, so this is safe to try.

Now the interesting part. We pick the bad deploy scenario, and switch.

With a real PagerLens set, the shop now fails on purpose. Errors and slowness, so an incident opens on the other side.

There are also workloads for PayBroker payments, ShopFront shoppers and the LLM gateway. And for TrustDesk, and the identity provider.

A schedule is paced against a steady clock. So a slow target makes ticks skip, instead of piling up. And a hard rate ceiling keeps the simulator from flooding anything by mistake.

## 7 am_eric
Now, a different kind of traffic: a simulated company. A company is a simulated employer for TrustDesk.

It invents employees, new hires and long-serving staff. And it sends requests of each type: vacations, sick leave and expenses.

We stop the workload, name the company acme, and choose the quick check.

Then we press preview. Preview shows exactly what would be sent. It is decided from a seed, and it sends nothing.

A quarter of the requests are built to break a typical policy. Too little notice, or no receipt, for example. So TrustDesk has something to judge.

Every name is invented, on example dot com.

## 8 am_eric
Last, the settings tab. In the fleet, you never type app addresses in.

ecoctl gives the simulator the seven app addresses. A target is looked up each time something starts. So a recreated app is followed automatically.

Here on a laptop, they all say not set.

Credentials are saved encrypted, and never shown again. Nothing secret is ever stored inside a city or a workload.

The app also remembers what was running, so a restart brings everything back.

For monitoring, it answers health checks, and Prometheus metrics. Let's ask it, from the terminal.
[pause 1]
The metrics show zero workloads running, because we stopped it. And the health check says okay. It also reports its own health to PagerLens.

Three roles control who can do what. Viewers only look. Operators run and edit simulations. Administrators also manage credentials and tokens.

## 9 af_bella
Let's wrap up what the CityPulse Simulator does.

It builds cities of sensors that speak real industrial protocols. It bursts them on demand. It runs scheduled traffic for the other apps. And it sends simulated employee requests to TrustDesk.

All of it comes from one app, with roles, a vault and metrics. It needs only Postgres. And the same files run headless, with no database at all.

Start with the guide, in the docs folder. Run it locally in five minutes. Then point it at the fleet, and watch the other apps come alive.
